Safety, infection control, tools, communication▶

Infection control: three levels

Cleaning removes visible debris with soap and water and always comes first. Disinfection kills most pathogens on non-porous surfaces using a hospital-grade, government-registered disinfectant, by immersion for the full labelled contact time. Sterilization destroys everything including spores, using an autoclave (steam under pressure), and is only needed for tools that penetrate the skin.

Order every time: clean, disinfect, then store in a closed, sanitary container. Porous single-use items (nail files, buffers, neck strips, wooden sticks) cannot be disinfected and are thrown out after one client.

Blood and body fluids

If skin is nicked: stop, clean the tool, and disinfect with a product effective against HIV and hepatitis B (tuberculocidal). Discard contaminated single-use items in the proper container, and put sharps like razor blades in a puncture-proof sharps container.

Antiseptic vs disinfectant

An antiseptic is used on living skin. A disinfectant is used on non-living surfaces and tools. They are not interchangeable.

WHMIS 2015 and the SDS

WHMIS 2015 follows the Globally Harmonized System. Every product has a Safety Data Sheet with 16 sections listing hazards, safe handling, first aid, and required PPE. Learn the pictograms: flame for flammable, the corrosion symbol for corrosive, the exclamation mark for an irritant.

PPE, draping, tools

Wear gloves for any colour, lightening, or chemical service, plus eye protection where needed. Drape a cutting cape for dry cutting, and a waterproof cape with a towel and neck strip for shampoo and chemical work. Know your tools: straight shears cut the line, thinning shears remove bulk not length, a razor gives soft tapered ends on wet hair, clippers handle short work, fades, and outlines.

Professional practice

Start with a consultation and analysis. Keep client record cards, protect client privacy, and use release forms for chemical services. Stay in scope: recognize a suspected medical condition and refer it, never diagnose or treat it.

High-yieldClean before you disinfect, disinfectants do not work through debris. Sterilization only for skin-penetrating tools. Blood needs an HIV and HBV effective disinfectant. The SDS has 16 sections. Gloves for all chemical services.
Common trapDo not sterilize combs and scissors, disinfect them. Never reuse a disposable item. A stronger-than-label disinfectant is not safer or more correct.
BHair and scalp care
Structure, analysis, disorders, products▶

Structure of the hair

Three layers: the cuticle (outer scales that protect and must open for chemicals to enter), the cortex (about 90% of the hair, holding melanin, strength, and elasticity, and where colour, perms, and relaxers act), and the medulla (the core, often absent in fine or naturally blonde hair).

Hair is the protein keratin. Peptide (end) bonds link amino acids into chains, and breaking them is permanent damage. Side bonds set the shape: disulfide (strong, sulfur, broken only by chemicals), hydrogen (physical, broken by water and heat), and salt (broken by a change in pH).

Melanin sets natural colour: eumelanin gives brown and black, pheomelanin gives red and yellow.

Growth cycle

Anagen is active growth (about 90% of scalp hair, lasting years). Catagen is a short transition. Telogen is the resting phase before shedding (about 10%).

The four analysis factors

  • Porosity: how fast hair absorbs. High porosity has a raised cuticle, absorbs fast, and over-processes easily. Low porosity resists and needs more time or heat.
  • Elasticity: healthy wet hair stretches about 50% and returns. Poor elasticity means damage and breakage risk.
  • Density: how many hairs per area (thin, medium, thick).
  • Texture: the diameter of one strand (fine, medium, coarse). Coarse hair is more resistant to chemical services.

Hair and skin sit at about pH 4.5 to 5.5. Alkaline products open the cuticle, acidic products close it.

Disorders: service or refer

Can service (non-contagious): pityriasis capitis simplex (dry dandruff), canities (grey), trichoptilosis (split ends), trichorrhexis nodosa (nodular breakage), monilethrix (beaded hair), fragilitas crinium (brittle hair), and psoriasis when it is not broken or severe.

Refer, do not service (contagious or infected): tinea capitis (ringworm, fungal), pediculosis capitis (head lice), scabies (mite), and furuncles or carbuncles (boils, bacterial).

Products and water

Shampoo cleans with a surfactant that has a water-loving head and an oil-loving tail. Clarifying shampoo strips buildup, medicated shampoo treats scalp issues. Conditioner is acidic to close the cuticle, and protein reconstructors temporarily strengthen damaged hair. Hard water carries minerals that cut lather, leave buildup, and can skew colour.

High-yieldThe cortex is where chemistry happens. Know the bond types and what breaks each. Anagen about 90%, telogen about 10%. Porosity and elasticity drive timing. Know which disorders require referral.
Common trapDensity is the amount of hair, texture is strand thickness, do not mix them. Never service a contagious condition. The medulla is not always present.
B+Scalp and hair disorders, deep dive
Your weak spot, drilled deep▶

How to read a scalp

Part the hair in several places under good light. Look for redness, scaling and flaking (note dry vs greasy), oiliness, pustules or crusts, broken hairs, bald patches, and signs of parasites (nits cemented to the shaft, live lice, burrows). Ask about itching, recent shedding, medications, products, and family history. Every time, ask one question: is it contagious, infected, or broken and inflamed? If yes, refer and do not service.

Bacterial (refer if infected)

  • Folliculitis: inflamed follicles with small pustules.
  • Furuncle: a single painful boil around a follicle.
  • Carbuncle: a cluster of connected boils.
  • Impetigo: contagious, honey-coloured crusts.

Fungal (refer)

  • Tinea capitis (ringworm): scaly ring-shaped patches, broken hairs, sometimes black dots.
  • Favus (tinea favosa): yellow, cup-shaped crusts called scutula.

Parasitic (refer)

  • Pediculosis capitis: head lice and nits.
  • Scabies: itch mite, intense itching that is worse at night.

Inflammatory, non-contagious (service gently, refer if severe or broken)

  • Pityriasis capitis simplex: dry dandruff.
  • Pityriasis steatoides: greasy, waxy dandruff.
  • Seborrheic dermatitis: red, greasy, scaly patches.
  • Psoriasis: silvery scaly plaques.
  • Eczema / contact dermatitis: reaction to an irritant or allergen.
  • Asteatosis: dry scalp from too little sebum.

Hair-shaft defects (structural, not contagious)

These can be serviced, with conditioning, trims, and gentle handling advised: trichoptilosis (split ends), trichorrhexis nodosa (nodes that break), monilethrix (beaded hair), fragilitas crinium (brittle hair), pili annulati (ringed hair), trichonodosis (knotted hair).

Hair loss and growth

  • Androgenic (pattern) alopecia: genetic and hormonal thinning.
  • Alopecia areata: round, smooth bald patches, autoimmune.
  • Alopecia totalis / universalis: loss of all scalp hair, or all body hair.
  • Telogen effluvium: diffuse shedding after stress, illness, or childbirth, usually temporary.
  • Traction alopecia: loss from tight styles, ponytails, and extensions.
  • Trichotillomania: hair loss from a pulling habit.
  • Hypertrichosis / hirsutism: excessive hair growth.
Quick differentialsNits are cemented to the shaft and do not brush off; dandruff flakes are loose. Alopecia areata patches are smooth with no scaling; tinea patches are scaly with broken hairs. Dry dandruff flakes are white and dry; steatoides is greasy and waxy.

Telling the look-alikes apart

Most scalp questions are not "name the disorder." They give you a client, one or two clues, and ask what you do. The trap options are always the close cousin. Learn the one clue that separates each pair.

Bald patches: the four that get confused
  • Alopecia areata: round, smooth patch, no scaling, no broken hairs. Autoimmune, can regrow. Serviceable.
  • Tinea capitis: scaly, ring-shaped, broken hairs, black dots. Fungal, contagious. Refer.
  • Traction alopecia: thinning at the hairline and temples, history of tight styles. Reversible early. Advise looser styles.
  • Trichotillomania: irregular patch, broken hairs of uneven lengths, a pulling habit. Behavioural.

Fast rule: smooth = areata, scaly + broken = tinea (refer), hairline + tight styles = traction, uneven broken lengths + habit = trichotillomania.

Red scaly scalp: the three that get confused
  • Psoriasis: thick silvery scales, sharply bordered plaques, often past the hairline. Non-contagious, service gently.
  • Seborrheic dermatitis: greasy yellowish red patches in oily zones (scalp, brows, sides of nose). Non-contagious.
  • Contact dermatitis: red, itchy, weepy, appears after a product and sits where the product touched. Allergen or irritant.

Fast rule: silvery and thick = psoriasis, greasy and yellow = seborrheic, "started after the colour" = contact dermatitis.

Bumps and pustules: bacterial cluster
  • Folliculitis: several inflamed follicles with small pustules.
  • Furuncle: one painful boil around a single follicle.
  • Carbuncle: a cluster of connected boils, the most severe.

All three are bacterial infections. If it is pustular, painful, or infected, refer and do not service.

Diffuse shedding vs patches

Telogen effluvium is diffuse shedding all over, usually two to four months after stress, illness, surgery, or childbirth, with no bald patches, and it is usually temporary. Do not confuse it with the defined patches of areata or the patterned thinning of androgenic alopecia.

Proceed, service gently, or refer

Every scalp scenario resolves to one of three answers. Learn which bucket each condition falls in and the questions answer themselves.

  • Refer, do not service (contagious, infected, or broken and inflamed): tinea capitis, favus, pediculosis, scabies, impetigo, folliculitis, furuncle, carbuncle, any open or oozing lesion.
  • Service gently with home-care advice (non-contagious surface conditions): dry and greasy dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, psoriasis, mild eczema, asteatosis. Refer out if severe, broken, or inflamed.
  • Service with care, manage the hair (structural shaft defects, not scalp disease): split ends, trichorrhexis nodosa, monilethrix, brittle or fragile hair. Advise against harsh chemical services on very fragile hair.
The scenario trapThe wrong answer is almost always "proceed carefully" or "you treat it yourself" on a condition that should be referred. If it is contagious, infected, or broken and inflamed, the answer is refer, every time, no matter how the question is worded.

Scalp treatments

Massage boosts circulation: effleurage is gentle stroking to relax, petrissage is kneading to stimulate. Use clarifying or gentle exfoliation for buildup, moisturizing for a dry scalp, and balancing care for oily. Medicated and antifungal products are a referral, not a salon fix.

The one rule to never missContagious, infected, or broken inflamed skin means refer and do not perform the service. Non-contagious dry, scaly, or structural conditions can be serviced gently with home-care advice, referring out if severe.
CCuts hair
Elevation, guides, lines, tools, technique▶

Elevation and the shape it makes

  • 0 degrees: blunt, one length, maximum weight.
  • About 45 degrees: graduation, building a weight line (stacking).
  • 90 degrees: uniform layers.
  • Over 90 up to 180: increasing length toward the exterior (long layers).

Guides and over-direction

A stationary guide brings sections to one fixed point, building length or weight there. A traveling guide moves with each section for even layers or graduation. Over-direction means combing hair away from its natural fall to a held position, which increases length in that direction and softens corners.

Cutting lines

  • Horizontal: builds weight, the base of blunt shapes.
  • Vertical: removes weight, the base of layers.
  • Diagonal forward: face-framing, longer at the front.
  • Diagonal back: stacking toward the back.

Control and checking

Work in clean sections and cross-check using partings opposite to how you cut. Use full even tension for precise straight lines, and less tension on curly hair, cowlicks, and the hairline. Curly hair shrinks as it dries, so cut it longer than the finished target.

Reference points and growth patterns

Key points: apex (highest), parietal ridge or crest (widest), occipital (back curve), the four corners, and the nape. Growth patterns like cowlicks, whorls, and widow's peaks need less tension and extra length.

Tools and techniques

Shears cut the line, point or notch cutting softens the ends, slide cutting reduces bulk along the strand, carving adds direction. A razor is for wet hair only and soft tapering, not fragile hair. Clipper-over-comb and scissor-over-comb blend short lengths. On clipper guards, a higher number leaves more length. A taper never reaches the skin, a fade blends to skin, named low, mid, or high by where the skin blend starts.

High-yieldMemorize the elevation results, the two guide types, and the four cutting lines. Cross-check the opposite way you cut. Account for curl shrinkage. Know taper vs fade.
Common trapHigh tension on wavy or curly hair cuts it too short once it springs up. Blunt cutting makes fine hair look fuller, so do not thin it.
DStyles hair
Wet sets, pin curls, rollers, thermal, products▶

What holds a style

Wet sets and thermal styling work on hydrogen bonds, a physical, temporary change that reverts with water or humidity. This is why a set drops in the rain.

Pin curls

Parts: base, stem, circle. The stem controls movement, no-stem is tight and firm, full-stem gives the most mobility. An open-center curl makes uniform waves, a closed-center curl is tighter with waves that get smaller. Base shapes: rectangular and arc bases give smooth waves, triangular bases prevent splits at the hairline, square bases give strong curl.

Rollers and volume

Choose a roller one size larger than the curl you want. On-base gives the most volume, half-base medium, off-base the least. Backcombing at the base builds volume, back-brushing blends the surface for a soft finish.

Thermal work

A round brush adds curl and volume, a paddle or flat brush smooths, a diffuser keeps curl and reduces frizz. Iron barrel size sets curl size. A hard press uses more heat and pressure and removes more curl than a soft press. Always use a heat protectant, and point the dryer nozzle down the shaft for shine.

Products

Gel gives firm hold and definition, mousse gives volume and light hold, pomade and wax add texture and shine, serum controls frizz and adds shine, hairspray is finishing hold.

High-yieldHydrogen bonds are the temporary bonds. The stem controls mobility. Open vs closed center. On-base for volume. Match the product to the job.
Common trapRemoving rollers before the hair is fully dry drops the set. Backcombing the ends causes breakage, keep it at the base.

Perm and relaxer chemistry▶

Perm chemistry

The waving lotion (a reducing agent) breaks disulfide bonds by adding hydrogen. The neutralizer then re-forms them by oxidation, usually with hydrogen peroxide or sodium bromate, locking in the new shape.

Cold (alkaline) wave: ammonium thioglycolate, pH about 8.5 to 9.5. Acid wave: glyceryl monothioglycolate, lower pH and gentler. A true acid wave is endothermic and needs an outside heat source, an exothermic wave makes its own heat from an added activator.

Rod size sets curl size. On-base placement adds volume. Croquignole wrapping goes ends to scalp, spiral wrapping runs along the rod for long hair. End papers prevent bent ends (fishhooks). A test curl shows the S-shape to confirm processing. Over-processed hair is frizzy and weak, under-processed hair is limp or straight.

Relaxer chemistry

Hydroxide (lye, sodium hydroxide): the strongest, highest pH (about 12 to 14), permanent through lanthionization, and irreversible. No-lye (guanidine hydroxide): gentler on the scalp but can leave calcium buildup and dryness. Thio: the mildest.

Base the scalp and hairline with protective cream, and do not pre-shampoo before a hydroxide relaxer, it can cause burning. Neutralize a relaxer with an acid-balanced neutralizing shampoo that lowers the pH, not with an oxidizer like a perm. On a virgin relaxer, apply to the most resistant hair first and keep product off the scalp early. On a retouch, only the new growth, since overlapping causes breakage.

High-yieldReduction breaks disulfide bonds, oxidation re-forms them. Cold vs acid wave chemistry. Hydroxide relaxers work by lanthionization and are neutralized by lowering pH, not by oxidation. Retouch new growth only.
Common trapDo not neutralize a hydroxide relaxer like a perm. Do not pre-shampoo before a relaxer. Never perm hair that has a hydroxide relaxer, it will break.
FAlters hair colour
Level, developers, colour law, lightening, correction▶

Level and tone

Level is depth, 1 (darkest) to 10 (lightest). Tone is warmth or coolness, the reflect sitting on the level.

Underlying pigment

Lightening exposes warm pigment in a fixed order, dark to light: red, red-orange, orange, yellow-orange, yellow, pale yellow. Around level 6 the underlying pigment is orange, near level 9 to 10 it is yellow to pale yellow. This pigment is exposed, not added.

Developers

  • 10 volume = 3%, mainly deposit.
  • 20 volume = 6%, grey coverage and 1 to 2 levels of lift.
  • 30 volume = 9%, 2 to 3 levels.
  • 40 volume = 12%, maximum lift, kept off the scalp.

Mixing developers averages the volume, so equal parts 20 and 40 give about 30. Use lower volumes on the scalp and higher volumes off-scalp in foils.

Worked math: developers and mixing

The exam turns these into "you need X, which do you use" and "you mixed Y and Z, what did you get." Work them the same way every time.

Volume to percentage

Divide the volume by about 3.3, or just memorize the four pairs: 10 = 3%, 20 = 6%, 30 = 9%, 40 = 12%. Each 10 volumes is roughly 3% peroxide. If a question gives you 6% and asks the volume, that is 20 volume.

Averaging two developers

Mixing equal parts averages the two volumes. Worked: equal parts 20 + 40 = (20 + 40) ÷ 2 = 30 volume. Equal parts 10 + 30 = (10 + 30) ÷ 2 = 20 volume. This only holds for equal parts; unequal ratios pull the result toward the larger share.

Levels of lift to developer

Match the lift you need to the volume: 1 level or grey coverage = 20 vol, 2 to 3 levels = 30 vol, maximum lift off-scalp = 40 vol. Worked: natural level 6 going to level 9 is 3 levels, so choose 30 volume on virgin hair, keeping 40 volume off-scalp only. Remember this is lift on natural pigment, not on artificial colour, because colour does not lift colour.

Two-step correction, start to finish

Case: level 4 box brown, client wants level 8 blonde. Step 1, oxidative tint will not lift it, so lighten with a lightener and the right developer off-scalp, processing to the target stage (level 8 sits at yellow underlying pigment). Step 2, once lifted to a clean yellow, tone with a violet-based toner to cancel the remaining yellow and land the blonde. Skipping step 2 leaves it brassy; toning before the lift is complete does nothing because a toner cannot fix under-lifted orange.

Case: over-lightened ends, client wants to go to level 6 brown. Step 1, pre-fill the porous ends with a warm (gold or copper) tone to replace the missing warm base pigment. Step 2, apply the level 6 target. Skipping the fill lets the cool pigment grab and turns the result dull or green.

Law of colour

Primaries are red, yellow, blue. Secondaries are orange, green, violet. To neutralize, use the complement: violet cancels yellow, blue cancels orange, green cancels red. Equal parts of a colour and its complement make a neutral brown.

Colour categories

  • Temporary: coats the surface, one shampoo.
  • Semi-permanent: no developer, deposit only, fades gradually.
  • Demi-permanent: low-volume developer, deposit only, no lift.
  • Permanent: ammonia plus developer, lifts and deposits, leaves a regrowth line.
  • High-lift: permanent, maximum lift with less deposit.

Ammonia raises pH, opens the cuticle, and activates the peroxide, MEA is an ammonia-free option with less lift. The developer both oxidizes the dye and lifts melanin. Remember: colour does not lift colour, so to go lighter on previously coloured hair you need a lightener or a colour remover.

Grey, lightening, and toning

More grey needs more natural (N) base, resistant grey may need pre-softening and 20 volume. When lightening, stop at the right stage, platinum needs pale yellow. A toner neutralizes residual warmth but cannot fix hair that is still under-lifted (still orange). A filler replaces missing warm pigment when taking porous or over-lightened hair darker, without it the result turns dull or green.

Correction and testing

Too dark: remove some artificial pigment, then re-tone. Too brassy: tone with the complement. Uneven: even the porosity or fill first. Hot roots mean the scalp over-lifted, a band means overlap. Always test: a patch (predisposition) test 24 to 48 hours ahead checks for allergy, a strand test predicts result and timing, and a metallic salt test (equal parts 20 volume peroxide and ammonia for about 30 minutes) warns of a reaction before any oxidative service. On a virgin lightening application, do the mid-lengths and ends first and the scalp last, because scalp heat processes faster.

High-yieldDeveloper volumes and percentages, the underlying pigment order, the neutralizing pairs, the colour categories, colour-does-not-lift-colour, fillers, and the three tests.
Common trapToning hair that is still orange will not work. Do not use 40 volume on the scalp. Do not overlap tint onto coloured ends. Do not skip the filler when darkening porous hair.

Extensions, wigs, hairpieces▶

Extensions

  • Fusion: heated keratin bonds.
  • Tape-in: adhesive wefts.
  • Sew-in (weft): sewn onto a braided track.
  • Micro-link (bead): clamped beads, no heat or glue.
  • Clip-in: temporary, the client removes them.

Too much tension over time causes traction alopecia. Fusion bonds come off with a bond remover.

Wigs and hairpieces

Human hair takes heat and colour, synthetic does not and needs cool-water care. Cap types: lace front (natural hairline), monofilament (scalp-like part), wefted (breathable and cheaper). Measure the head, secure the wig on a canvas block, and note that ventilating means hand-tying hair into the base or lace.

High-yieldMatch each extension method to how it attaches. Synthetic hair will not take heat or colour. Know the cap types and what ventilating means.

Records, business models, retail, scope▶

Records and consent

Client record or service cards let you reproduce a formula and avoid repeating a past reaction. Use release and consent forms for chemical services, and keep client information confidential.

Pay models

  • Commission: a percentage of the services you perform, usually as an employee.
  • Chair (booth) rental: you pay fixed rent and run your own independent business.
  • Salary: fixed pay.

Business basics

Service revenue comes from performing services, retail revenue from selling product. Retail is priced by adding a markup to cost. Cost of goods is the product used per service. Control inventory so you never run out. Rebooking builds retention, and ethical retailing means recommending what actually suits the client. Stay in scope and refer suspected medical conditions.

Part 2: The hands-on practical

The written exam is only half of BC certification. The practical is a separate, in-person, hands-on assessment booked through North Pacific (about $500). It tests whether you can actually perform to standard, safely and on time, not what you know on paper.

Confirm the format from the sourceThe exact stations, time limits, what you bring (kit, mannequin or live model), and how each part is scored are set by North Pacific and SkilledTradesBC and can change. Get the current candidate information package from them and prep to that. The guidance below is general practice structure, not their official station list.

What a hairstylist practical assesses

Across most hairstylist practicals, the assessed competencies map to the same trade standard the written exam uses. Expect to be judged on:

  • Client consultation and analysis: reading hair and scalp, confirming the service, checking for contraindications before you start.
  • Haircutting: sectioning, elevation and guide control, even lines, cross-checking, a clean finished shape.
  • Chemical texture: safe, correct perm or relaxer application, timing, neutralizing, protecting skin and hair.
  • Hair colour: mixing, application technique, sectioning, coverage, clean work with no bleeding or staining.
  • Styling and finishing: blow-dry, thermal or set work to a polished, intentional result.
  • Sanitation, infection control, and safety: clean tools and station, disinfection, draping, blood-spill readiness, PPE.
  • Time management and professionalism: finishing each service in the allotted time, organized, calm, client-focused.

Where candidates lose marks

The avoidable failsSanitation slips (dirty tools, no disinfection, cross-contamination), poor draping or client protection, uneven sectioning, running out of time, skipping the consultation or scalp check, and messy chemical application (overlap, staining, poor timing). Most of these are habits, not talent, so drill them until they are automatic.

Self-assessment rubric

Practise each service on a mannequin or model and score yourself honestly, 1 (rough) to 5 (exam-ready). Anything under 4 is a rebook-to-practise item. Retake it in a few days and watch the numbers climb.

Prep plan for the practical

  1. Get the official package from North Pacific and list the exact services, times, and kit required.
  2. Set up a station that mirrors the exam: same tools, same disinfection routine, timer running.
  3. Time every practice service. Speed with clean work is the thing being tested; practise to beat the clock with margin.
  4. Make sanitation muscle memory. Disinfect, drape, and set up the same way every single time so you never think about it on the day.
  5. Rehearse the consultation out loud, including the scalp and contraindication check, so it is second nature.
  6. Do full dress rehearsals of each service end to end, then score with the rubric above until everything sits at 4 to 5.

She already reads hair and scalp well and passed the written comfortably. The practical rewards clean, safe, timed repetition. Rehearse the way you will be tested and it becomes routine.
