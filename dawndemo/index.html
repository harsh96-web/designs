The vision

A small complete thing.

Most games ask for hours. DAWN asks for thirty seconds — and gives you a complete sunrise back.

For
casual mobile players, students with five minutes to fill, and fans of small focused games like LIMBO or Alto's Adventure.
Who
want a short, focused game where every press counts — not a long one that takes hours.
The
DAWN
Is a
30-second one-button game about running toward the light.
That
  • gives you a full night-to-sunrise journey in every run
  • uses just one button: tap to jump, hold longer to jump higher
  • speeds up the music as the game gets harder, so you feel the tension
  • adds optional embers to collect for a tougher challenge
Unlike
Geometry Dash (long levels you have to memorize), Flappy Bird (no story, no progress), or Subway Surfers (busy screen, pushy ads).
Ours
is the only short one-button game with a full sunrise story in every run — and you can install it on your phone like a real app.

What makes DAWN different

Built for feeling, not grinding.

01
Complete story in every run
Night, dawn, sunrise — three phases in thirty seconds. You don't have to play for hours to feel something.
02
Music that speeds up with you
The chiptune ramps from 100 to 160 BPM across the run. Tension you can hear, not just see.
03
Optional embers at three heights
Casual players ignore them. Skilled players chase the highest. A hidden second challenge inside the same 30 seconds.
04
Neon outlines for visibility
Playtesters couldn't see obstacles at night. Seeing what might kill you matters more than keeping the art pure.

Design decisions

Balancing techniques applied.

Every detail was chosen so players feel a fair, focused, satisfying 30 seconds.

Tuning by feel
Scroll speed grows from 4.5 to 8.5 across a run. Gap-narrowing softened from 35% to 25% so the late game stays beatable.
Forgiveness margin
Visual radius 9px, hit radius 7px. A 2-pixel buffer makes close calls feel fair.
No hard stuff early
Multi-spike clusters and tall walls only appear after the first 5 seconds.
Easy common, hard rare
~75% of spikes are singles. Triple clusters appear only 8% of the time.
Different jumps, different obstacles
Spikes need a quick tap, walls need a long hold. The player stays sharp.
Music that speeds up
Chiptune ramps 100 → 160 BPM by sunrise. Rising tension is felt, not just seen.
Tell the player how they died
The death screen says "You hit a spike" or "You hit a wall." Failure feels clear, not random.
Neon outlines for visibility
Switched from procedural rim lighting to warm neon. Clarity over aesthetic purism when readability is gameplay-critical.
Performance is fairness
Replaced expensive shadowBlur with multi-stroke glow + capped mobile rendering at 2× resolution. A laggy game cannot be fair.
Less on the welcome
Removed the 4-card tutorial. The in-game first-run hint covers what's left. A 30-second game teaches itself.
Optional embers
Three heights to collect. Skilled players chase the highest — a hidden second skill curve.
Cross-platform
Installable as a PWA. Phone players get the same game as desktop players.

Reading

Articles that shaped the design.

01
Balancing Multiplayer Competitive Games
David Sirlin · sirlin.net
Takeaway: balance isn't about everything being equal — it's about every choice mattering. DAWN has just one button, but every press is a real decision: when, how long.
02
How to Make a Punishing Game Feel Fair
Mark Brown · Game Maker's Toolkit
Takeaway: punishing games need three things: instant restart, clear cause of death, and a forgiveness margin. DAWN has all three.
03
Difficulty Modes and Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment
Ernest Adams · Game Developer
Takeaway: difficulty should rise smoothly, not in sudden jumps. DAWN ramps speed by 0.10 px/frame each second of survival.
